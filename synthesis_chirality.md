# Chirality in the Sedenion Framework: Forced, Protected, Carried

**Revision 1.0 — closed.** An entry point, not a sixth paper. This note states the framework's chirality result end to end, in the plain register, citing Papers I–V for every claim and reproducing no proof. Read this first; open whichever of the five closed doors the reader needs next.

## 1. The forcing

Paper I derives the Standard Model's gauge group, exact hypercharges, and a native chirality mechanism from a single algebraic object — the mediator direction in the octonions and the idempotent it fixes in $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ — with two independent implementations agreeing on every checkpoint. Paper II takes this matter content into the framework's own scale-line dynamics: a domain-wall construction localizes eight modes whose gauge content, $\{\mathbf{1}_{y_\ell}\}\oplus\{\mathbf{3}_{y_q}\}$, is *necessarily* chiral under unbroken $SU(3)\times U(1)_Y$ — forced by Schur's lemma acting on the wall's own $\mathbf{3}$ and the fact that $\mathbf{3}\not\cong\bar{\mathbf{3}}$, independent of any spectral detail beyond the threshold theorem's own existence. A companion gate theorem shows no operator can mix the two chirality columns while preserving weak isospin, so fermion mass requires electroweak breaking — re-derived, not assumed.

What Paper II also proves, and what everything below depends on: exactly four gauge-compatible orientation structures exist, $\pm J_L\oplus\pm J_Q$, splitting into a Standard-Model-shaped class and its mirror, with the dynamics commuting with all four. The wall is chiral; *which* mirror it is remains, at this point, undetermined.

## 2. The protection

Paper III asks whether anything the framework derives selects between the two classes, and proves that nothing does. The proof is a closed, four-part partition over the entire legal operator algebra: symmetric perturbations vanish identically on the wall (the mode-reality lemma); every $J_0$-odd operator's alignment vanishes by a trace identity (the trace lemma); the framework's own derived-sign operators are diagonal, hence per-line scalars with no alignment content (the per-line theorem); and every operator that *could* align turns out to carry a gauge or convention sign rather than a derived one (sign-provenance, closed under a verified gauge-orbit sweep). Two plausible theorem-candidates — that mere realness protects the orientation, and that a specific derived operator could break it — are shown false by direct construction and are reported as such. A corollary locks the framework's one genuinely derived sign, the thermal arrow, into the same protected sector: the arrow cannot reach the orientation at any order.

The result is not that the orientation is unknown — it is that the orientation is *provably unknowable from anything the framework derives*: an initial-condition datum, not a gap awaiting a cleverer operator.

## 3. The carrier

If nothing derived chooses, the only remaining candidate is the framework's own settling dynamics — and Papers IV and V locate exactly where that dynamics could live and exactly what it can and cannot do.

Paper IV finds settling's only named algebraic home: the zero-divisor structure of $\mathbb{S}$, the one structure that necessarily mixes both Cayley–Dickson halves. It maps this structure combinatorially into seven "box-kites," identifies an exact automorphism (read, on verified facts, as the algebra's own PT operation) splitting them $3+4$, and shows the orientation-bearing sector is precisely the four PT-odd kites — a finite, mapped landscape, not a metaphor.

Paper V transports this landscape to the gauge side and tests it to a conclusion. The transport is canonical on exactly one channel: the mediator fiber's single bit maps onto $\pm J_Q$, while every other channel is doubly excluded (gauge-incompatible, and where relevant, swept to its antipode by the same $SO(3)$ remnant that fixes the mediator fiber) — the lepton-relative sign sits outside every invariant space tested. The CAR quantization closes on this structure: four addressed vacua, thermal states orientation-blind at every temperature. Finally, testing Becoming's own candidate settling mechanism directly: it bifurcates exactly into direction-free members (orientation-contentless, though not structureless) and anchored members (contentful, carrying the mirror bit through a fully verified chain) — but nothing in the corpus supplies which point of the locus anchors.

**Settling can carry the orientation. It cannot author it.**

## 4. What remains

Two readings of this verdict are both live, and the framework does not adjudicate between them. Either this is the deepest result the corpus has produced — a genuine proof that a physical orientation can be an unprovable initial condition, mathematics catching itself in the act of building the walls around its own limits — or it is a precise, honest way of saying "not derivable from the tools assembled here," with the true mechanism simply not yet found. Nothing below decides which.

**The anchor-supplier question** (REG-13): any selector would have to live outside Paper III's entire audited, symplectic jurisdiction — a located address, not yet a candidate.
**The preservation lemma** (REG-14): needed to upgrade the non-Bogoliubov trichotomy from citation to theorem; supplied nowhere.
**The lepton-relative sign**: carriable only at the price of breaking the $SO(3)$ remnant — the deepest symmetry surviving every prior stage of this program — whether nature pays that price is untouched.
**Reading B**: everything above is internal chirality on the scale line; identification with spacetime's $\gamma^5$ awaits a spacetime program not yet begun.
**The $e_R$/generation boundary**: no half of this matter content is a full generation, unmoved by anything above.

None of these are computations waiting to run — they are gated behind programs that have not started. A synthesis that states them open is complete; one that waited for them would never be written.

Whether an anchor's history in fact selects a mirror class is a question about the world, not about this corpus's mathematics, and is not addressed here.
